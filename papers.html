P-027

The Dissipation Budget Law: Port-Hamiltonian Streams and the Conservation Architecture for Depth

Depth is rented, never owned — every standard residual stream pays norm and gradient rent a normalizer refunds after the fact. The port-Hamiltonian stream makes the guarantees structural: Cayley transport (exactly orthogonal, volume-free to 10^-14 at any step size), metered dissipation (the only volume-contracting channel — expressivity becomes a readable budget line), and ports as the only norm injection. Measured: zero bound violations in 7,200 runs while the control explodes to 10^43; gradient metering through 240 layers; a rank-2 readout at depth 120 scoring 98.1% against the rescued control’s 38.3% (held-out, three seeds). Rev 1.1 answers an external audit — four harness defects fixed, a 13-assertion law-test suite, and an end-to-end training experiment (E6) complete: trained to baseline parity at depth 240 with no normalizer anywhere (97.3% ± 0.3 held-out; spirals-2 open, 57.6%). Rev 1.2 makes the normalization comparison precise — §4.6 states exactly what LayerNorm guarantees (a per-sample layer statistic) and what it does not (any operator bound), reads the trained-model growth numbers as the type distinction made concrete (LN 51.0× vs PH 31.8× at depth 240), and ships the trunk-normalized follow-up ablation flag-gated in the harness. Integrator choice is normalization choice.

Groupthink has a formula. A collective of temperature-sampling agents reading a shared board bifurcates at K_c = T(1−λ) — coupling and temperature trade one-for-one, with no useful middle. The Green’s Function of Context: Positional Recall as an Inverse Design Problem

Context rot is the Green’s function of a positional kernel nobody designed. The recall profile is the kernel smeared by noise (0.037); the lost-in-the-middle dip lands exactly on its closed form (2771 = 2771); recency windows collapse with load exactly as the extreme-value law computes; and the monotonicity theorem says uniform recall is impossible with timescales alone — it takes a register. The register-plus-slope design buys flat recall at a 90% floor with order resolution priced by the window-order law (0.92 → 0.60 over half a decade to three). The cure for the middle is an inverse problem, minutes of compute.

P-025

The Collapse Law: Ceilings, Hysteresis, and the Recovery Threshold for Timeout-Retry Systems

Congestion collapse, priced — and audited. The good steady state of a timeout-retry system dies at a tangency with a closed form (λc = μθρ*²/(1+θρ*)), and the collapsed state survives on the retry budget itself, dying only below λr = μ/R — the maximum and the endpoint of one fixed-point curve, λ = μρ(1−q)/(1−q^R). New in revision 1.1: a derived bistability boundary — the hysteresis loop exists only when (R−1)θ > 2 — and its unlimited-retry limit measured: a collapsed system at 5% load with R = ∞ grows its queue seventeen-fold instead of draining; restart is the only exit. Multi-seed re-measurement (5–24 seeds, error bars): tipping-probability curves that saturate exactly at the ceiling, queue caps inside the horizon shown to eliminate the crater (loop gain q_L·R < 1), and the v1.0 single-seed claims — the −36% backoff resonance, the 81% jitter margin, the horizon "crossing" — withdrawn in Appendix B. The paper now documents its own audit.
