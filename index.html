Software the world is missing, specified before anyone builds it.

p-rick hunts for genuine gaps in the software landscape — categories with no incumbents, problems with large audiences, theories nobody has written down — and does the research to specify them: gap verification against everything that exists, formal models, architectures, evaluation designs, and honest confrontation with the reasons each gap has survived. Every paper ships with a PDF and a plain-language essay. Research first; products later.

Papers

Seven arcs. Series VII studies collapse — the critical thresholds where everyday infrastructure fails abruptly: dependency resolution as a satisfiability phase transition (P-024), the retry storm’s capacity ceiling and recovery threshold (P-025), and the credential cascade’s concentration separatrix (P-026) — three collapse phenomena, three derived laws, three harnesses. Series VI is the program’s quantitative turn — laws rather than specifications: the reproduction number of dependency-borne compromise (P-021), the closed-form cache admission threshold (P-022), and the generation-loss drift law for embedding corpora (P-023) — each a theorem, a validation harness, and figures that regenerate from code in the repository. Series V researches promises — the guarantees software implies and never has to honor: that withdrawal propagates (the revocation protocol), that stored facts stay true (epistemic half-life), that dormant software will wake (dormancy engineering), and that independent things fail independently (blast radius engineering). Series IV researches assumptions — the quiet premises software runs on: that complexity can be destroyed rather than relocated (the complexity ledger), that a body has one owner (delegated operation), that the user's senses are always online (modality failover), that every number is a point (the uncertain document), and that defaults are neutral (the defaults ledger). Series III researches continuity — what systems lose over time: vendor death (the afterlife of devices), human biological time (circadian orchestration), maintainer mortality (the bus factor protocol), and behavioral extinction (model conservation). Series II turns to systems gaps: behavior under scarcity, storage that remembers, the scheduling of human attention, and one machine across many devices. Series I specified user-owned data infrastructure — the personal event bus, consumer entitlements as dead capital, and the n=1 cost-of-living index. Each paper verifies its gap against the existing landscape before proposing anything; the method is on its own page.

P-021

The Reproduction Number of Code

securitynetworksepidemiology

Compromised packages are epidemics on the dependency DAG — and the spectral threshold the industry computes on registries is exactly zero (the matrix is nilpotent), while the true threshold is the branching law R₀ʲ = T·⟨d⟩. Below degree exponent 2 — where the real registries live — the safe zone vanishes as the registry grows. Herd pinning budgets, the two-step half-life of a registry yank, and the mathematics nobody ran, derived and validated.

P-022

The Admission Law

cachingperformanceclosed-form

Cache admission thresholds, tuned by folklore for a decade, written as a formula: τ* = (1−f)W / (H·Bα). The threshold tracks stable evidence rather than pollution; one-timers tax the unfiltered cache at f/(1−f) of its capacity; the door legally disables itself when it stops paying. Validated within 1% where the choice matters, gains to 137% at the flat-and-dirty corner.

P-023

Generation Loss

AI-infraretrievaldrift

Vector corpora drift between encoder upgrades by a law: D(g) = 1−λg, with λ split into an isometry channel (harmless in high dimension and removable exactly by a landmark Procrustes refresh) and a distortion channel (the entire real cost, un-alignable). The re-embedding calendar becomes arithmetic: two probes, one matrix solve, and the decision derives itself.

About

p-rick is an independent research program, not a product. Its subject is missing software: categories where a real problem meets a large audience and no incumbent — verified against the landscape before anything is proposed, not asserted after. Every paper states its gap as a falsifiable claim, grades the evidence honestly (strong / partial / weak), and confronts the strongest objections to its own thesis.

The program runs research-first: papers before products, always. The bar, the exclusions (no toy projects, no deterministic wrappers), the grading, and the red-team discipline are specified on the method page. Work is tracked in agents.md — an automatic session ledger recording what was done, when, and by which agent, so nobody has to remember or ask how long anything took.

Series I (user-owned data and the economics of inattention) began inside srivtx/pocketveto and moved here when the program outgrew its host. Series II (systems gaps), Series III (continuity — what systems lose over time: capability, maintainers, behavior, and human time), and Series IV (assumptions — the quiet premises software runs on, and what breaks when they are false) were researched, written, and published from this repository. Everything is open: sources, PDFs, essays, and the RSS feed.
